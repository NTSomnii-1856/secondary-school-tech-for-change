Year 7 Arduino and invention

Students move from their earlier Scratch work into text-based code that controls a circuit. They learn how inputs, decisions and outputs produce a useful response, then design a case and explain the evidence behind their choices.

Before Christmas: ICT, programming, data and digital/3D design. Christmas–April: the major physical competition invention. May: a fresh exhibition brief. Final month: enterprise and fundraising.

Proposed Week 6: Build a signal people can read. The existing Python game-club planner remains an available alternative until the teacher chooses. Existing lesson pages remain available. Future map entries are planned learning, not links to resources already made.

Planning notes

  • 36 teaching weeks, two 40-minute periods per week; two additional 60-minute community sessions. Week numbers are teaching weeks and must be matched to local holidays.
  • The selected ICT toolkit is explicitly taught and checked before Christmas. January to April is the major D&T competition project. May starts a distinct exhibition brief; the final four teaching weeks focus on enterprise and fundraising.
  • Humans, Animals and Nature are represented across the class. Teams choose a bounded need from evidence; the whole autumn does not share a gardening theme.
  • Tinkercad Circuits supports Arduino text code in C++. Wokwi supports Pico with MicroPython. Pico is a microcontroller; it is not a simulation of a full Raspberry Pi running Linux. The Raspberry Pi Foundation online Python editor is a coding editor, not hardware simulation.
  • Begin simulations without assuming students own hardware. Before Christmas, CAD is a digital skill. During the D&T phases, schedule actual physical materials, making and testing; simulation alone does not demonstrate physical manufacture or real-world reliability.
  • Keep one main platform per year. Starter circuits and explained boilerplate reduce setup load; students must still make an independent change and explain it. Year 8 learns the differences between CPython, MicroPython machine.Pin and Raspberry Pi GPIO libraries before transferring code.
  • Learn defines new ideas from zero. Your turn contains building and testing. Quick reference uses coding-topic headings. Independent problems have meaningful constraints, rubric criteria and test evidence; Level up remains optional.
  • These are local selected outcomes informed by Cambridge, Pearson, England KS3 and IB design/ATL. They do not claim completion of several full programmes or use invented awarding-body objective codes. Detailed stage alignment needs the school’s licensed frameworks.
  • Use practical portfolio evidence and individual explanations. A prepared lesson is not evidence that a class has completed it. Competition placing and money raised are not assessment grades.
  • Confirm competition rules, deadline and equipment early in the spring project. If the event is later, complete a judged school demonstration by April and retain the entry package.
  • Year 9 direction for later agreement: applied AI and data, model evaluation/bias, automation and an independently managed impact project. AI literacy stays present in Years 7–8; specialist AI is a later progression, not a promise of a full Year 9 map here.
